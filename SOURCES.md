## Review completed on this version

Reviewed all 16 challenges, four lessons, answer feedback, welcome/final screens, and shield text for clarity and age appropriateness:

- Children's screens contain no sexual health terminology, reproductive anatomy, graphic descriptions, frightening imagery, or shame.
- “Immune system” is immediately explained as the body's team of defenders. Cancer is described briefly as an illness where some cells grow out of control.
- HPV is described as a common virus that can cause certain cancers later in life, not as a certainty that someone will get cancer.
- HPV vaccination helps prevent several cancers, never every cancer. It is not described as treatment for existing illness.
- Age 9 is a possible starting age, not a claim that all children must start at 9. Adult information separately gives the routine ages 11–12 recommendation and directs scheduling questions to a healthcare professional.
- Shields and training are identified as learning symbols. Neither points nor finishing the game mean someone is vaccinated or protected. Vaccination is not instant or perfect protection.
- Wrong answers invite another try without losing points. No speed, health status, or personal information is requested.

This is an editorial review, not a clinical sign-off. A real 9–12-year-old playtest, supervised by a caregiver, is still needed to confirm reading level, comprehension, enjoyment, and the approximate 3–5 minute duration. Avoid gathering names or other identifying information during feedback.
